# Bharat Marketplace: Seller Health and Order Economics Analytics

An end-to-end analytics project on a simulated online marketplace serving Tier 2 and Tier 3 India: **SQL** for the analysis, **Python** for statistics and charts, **Power BI** for the dashboard.

> **About the data.** The dataset is synthetic and generated by `python/generate_data.py` with a fixed random seed. It is not data from any real company. The assumptions behind it are listed in [`docs/methodology.md`](docs/methodology.md). The project shows the method (framing, metric definitions, SQL, statistics, dashboard design), so the findings below describe the simulation.

## Business problem

A marketplace that wins on price in smaller cities lives or dies on three things: whether new sellers start selling and keep selling, how many orders come back undelivered (RTO, return to origin), and what it costs to move each parcel. This project answers five questions a category or seller-growth team would ask.

1. How fast is GMV growing, and does order quality hold as volume grows?
2. Where do new sellers drop out of the funnel, and which onboarding channel brings sellers who actually sell?
3. How long do sellers stay active after their first order?
4. What drives RTO, and what would reducing it be worth?
5. How much does logistics cost by city tier and courier, and how dependent is GMV on a few sellers?

## Key findings

**1. Growth is steep, with a festive spike and a December cool-off.** Monthly GMV grows from Rs 0.2 lakh in January to Rs 62 lakh in October, jumps 84% month on month in October, peaks at Rs 68 lakh in November, and falls 26% in December. Order quality stays flat while volume grows: RTO sits at 17.5 to 18.6% of shipped orders from March to December ([chart](reports/figures/01_gmv_trend.png)).

**2. Field sales and referrals activate far more sellers.** Overall 37.4% of registered sellers reach 5 or more orders within 30 days of their first order. Field sales reaches 49.4% and social / ads only 31.6%. The gap opens at the first step: 95% of field-sales sellers list a product against 74% of social / ads sellers ([chart](reports/figures/02_activation_by_channel.png)).

**3. A quarter of sellers have stopped selling by month three.** Average retention is 74.7% three months after the first order (February to August cohorts) and falls to roughly half by month six. Today 34.9% of sellers who ever sold are dormant, and 8.9% are at risk, meaning their last-30-day orders fell by half or more ([chart](reports/figures/03_cohort_retention.png)).

**4. Cash on delivery and distance drive RTO.** RTO is 18.2% of shipped orders overall. COD runs at 25.5% against 8.3% for prepaid. The worst cell is Tier 3 COD at 28.1%, the best is Tier 1 prepaid at 5.4%. In a logistic regression, COD multiplies the odds of RTO by 3.5, a Tier 3 destination by 1.8, and delivery taking 7 or more days by 1.6 (all p < 0.001, [chart](reports/figures/05_rto_odds_ratios.png), [heatmap](reports/figures/04_rto_heatmap.png)).

**5. Logistics eats a quarter of GMV in Tier 3.** Shipping cost is Rs 53 per order in Tier 1 and Rs 70 in Tier 3, or 16.7% against 25.7% of GMV. In Tier 3 the in-house network costs Rs 59 per order against Rs 87 for the most expensive partner courier ([chart](reports/figures/07_logistics_cost.png)).

**6. GMV is concentrated.** The top 10% of sellers deliver 51.3% of GMV and the top 20% deliver 69.1% ([chart](reports/figures/06_seller_pareto.png)).

### What this suggests (illustrative)

- **Push prepaid in Tier 3.** If 10 percentage points of Tier 3 COD orders moved to prepaid and behaved like existing prepaid buyers, about 680 RTO orders would be avoided, saving roughly Rs 0.7 lakh in shipping and keeping about Rs 1.9 lakh of GMV. This is an upper-end estimate; a prepaid incentive test would show the real effect.
- **Route more Tier 2 and 3 volume through the cheapest network** where delivery promises allow it.
- **Copy the field-sales onboarding playbook** for categories with high RTO and low seller activation, and fix listing support for social / ads sellers.
- **Run a save campaign on the 162 at-risk sellers.** They still average 87 lifetime orders each, so they are worth more than the average new seller.
- **Cap delivery time.** Orders delivered in 7 or more days have clearly higher RTO, so a delivery-time guardrail per courier and lane is worth setting.

## How to run

```bash
git clone <your-repo-url>
cd bharat-marketplace-seller-analytics
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate        # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_pipeline.py
```

The pipeline takes under a minute. It regenerates the data, builds a local SQLite database, runs all nine queries, writes the charts and tables to `reports/`, and exports the Power BI files. Python 3.10 or newer is needed. SQLite ships with Python, so no database install is required.

To run one query by hand, open `data/marketplace.db` in any SQLite client (DB Browser for SQLite works well) and paste a file from `sql/analysis/`.

## SQL queries

| File | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| `01_monthly_kpis.sql` | GMV, orders, AOV, RTO and MoM growth by month |
| `02_seller_activation_funnel.sql` | Register to list to first order to activated, by channel |
| `03_seller_cohort_retention.sql` | Share of each first-order cohort still selling after 0 to 6 months |
| `04_rto_drivers.sql` | RTO by tier, payment mode and delivery speed, ranked by GMV lost |
| `05_seller_concentration.sql` | Pareto split of GMV across seller deciles |
| `06_logistics_cost_by_tier.sql` | Shipping cost per order and as a share of GMV, by tier and courier |
| `07_category_and_discount.sql` | Category scorecard |
| `08_discount_bands.sql` | Basket value and RTO by discount depth |
| `09_seller_health_segments.sql` | Star, Growing, Stable, At risk and Dormant sellers |

## Power BI dashboard

Open [`powerbi/README.md`](powerbi/README.md) for the step-by-step build: load the six CSVs from `data/processed/`, set the relationships, paste the DAX, import the theme, and reconcile your cards against `reconciliation_checks.csv` before designing visuals. The four pages are Executive overview, Seller health, Logistics and RTO, and Category and pricing.

## Limitations and next steps

- The data is simulated, so the effects are a function of the assumptions. Swap in real order, seller and logistics tables with the same columns and the whole pipeline runs unchanged.
- Add price elasticity and competitor price data to test discount and pricing strategy.
- Add a seller churn model once real behavioural features are available.
- Schedule the pipeline and publish the Power BI report to the Service with a daily refresh.
